# AI readiness

Structured data, `llms.txt`, AI-crawler rules, and the readiness report — the
machine-readable half of being findable.

## The rule everything here follows

**Extract, never author.**

Everything on this page is derived from content your site already has. Nothing
is generated by a language model, nothing is invented, nothing calls out to a
service. If the information is not on the page, no tag is emitted for it.

That is a deliberate limit. A confident wrong answer in structured data is
worse than no answer, because it is machine-readable and taken at face value.

## Structured data

Every page carries a schema.org graph describing what it is:

| Type | Says |
|---|---|
| **Organization** (or your chosen type) | Who runs this site — name, logo, profiles elsewhere |
| **WebSite** | The site itself, and where its search lives |
| **WebPage** | This particular page |
| **BreadcrumbList** | Where this page sits in the site's hierarchy |
| **Article** | On blog posts — headline, dates, author |
| **FAQPage** | Where the page genuinely has questions and answers |

This is what lets Google show your business details in a knowledge panel, and
what lets an AI assistant answer "who is this" with something correct.

### Breadcrumbs are declared, not drawn

The BreadcrumbList states the hierarchy that already exists in WordPress. It
**does not add a visible breadcrumb trail** to your page. Your design does not
have one, and adding it would break the promise that the design is unchanged.

### Where the business details come from

**SEO & Sharing** — name, type, logo, profiles. If your original site already
carried a schema.org block, its business details were adopted at conversion.

Page-level types from the original are ignored deliberately; only the business
identity is adopted. The *type* is never guessed from content, which is why
the settings screen asks.

### With Yoast or Rank Math installed

Visual Edit emits **nothing of its own** and instead adds its pieces to their
graph.

Two separate graphs on one page do not error — but they produce duplicate
Organization and WebPage entities with conflicting details, which is exactly
the ambiguity structured data exists to remove, and it can cost rich-result
eligibility. One graph, jointly built.

## FAQ structured data

Questions and answers on a page are published as `FAQPage` data — which is
what lets Google show them directly and lets an assistant quote them.

### The shapes that are recognised

1. `<details><summary>Question</summary> answer </details>`
2. `<dl><dt>Question</dt><dd>Answer</dd></dl>`
3. A heading ending in `?`, followed by one or two paragraphs

**The question mark is the signal** in the third shape. It is what keeps
ordinary headings out.

Markup that renders as an FAQ but is not one of these — a `<div>` with a class
named "faq-question", or a `<button>`-based accordion — looks perfect on the
page and is **completely invisible** to this. The failure is silent, which is
why the shapes are documented.

### Two questions minimum

A page needs at least two real questions before anything is published.

This threshold comes from real content. One page yielded five genuine
questions. Another yielded exactly one: *"Ready for a clearer strategy?"* —
which is a call to action above a button, not a question. Publishing that as
an FAQ would have been an invented fact.

Answers shorter than 20 characters are dropped for the same reason.

### It follows your edits

Edit an FAQ — through the [questions editor](../guide/repeating-items.md) or
by hand — and the structured data updates on the next page load. There is nothing to regenerate.

## `llms.txt`

A plain-text summary of your site at `/llms.txt`, in the emerging convention
for making a site legible to AI assistants:

```
# Your Business

> Your site's description

## Pages

- [About](https://…/about/): what the page is about
…

## Posts

- [Latest article](https://…/post/): its excerpt
```

Generated live from your actual pages and their descriptions. Nothing to
maintain, and it cannot drift out of date.

Pages you have hidden from search are excluded, so it never contradicts your
sitemap.

**Limit: 200 entries per section.** If you have more, the file says so and
points at the sitemap — a truncated list that stays silent about being
truncated reads as "this is everything", which would be a lie.

## AI-crawler rules

One switch in **SEO & Sharing**: whether AI assistants are explicitly welcome.

When on, `robots.txt` names them and allows them:

`GPTBot` · `ChatGPT-User` · `OAI-SearchBot` · `ClaudeBot` · `Claude-User` ·
`Claude-SearchBot` · `PerplexityBot` · `Perplexity-User` · `Google-Extended` ·
`Applebot-Extended` · `CCBot` · `Bytespider` · `meta-externalagent`

Plus a pointer to your `llms.txt`.

**Allow rather than disallow**, because a converted site's owner generally
wants to be found. Naming them explicitly rather than relying on a blanket
rule makes it a decision on the record, and gives the switch something real to
turn off.

One switch rather than thirteen, because nobody wants to make this decision
per bot.

> **If you use Rank Math**, it replaces `robots.txt` with its own editor and
> these lines will not appear. The readiness report detects this and shows you
> the lines to paste, rather than failing quietly.

## The readiness report

**Visual Edit → SEO & AI Readiness.** A count of problems appears next to the
menu item.

### It only reports

**Nothing on this screen changes a page, and nothing ever will.**

A one-click "fix" that inserts a heading, invents alt text or reorders your
headings would break the byte-for-byte promise — silently, on the one thing
you paid for. Every finding names the problem and stops there.

### What it checks, per page

| Check | Level | |
|---|---|---|
| Exactly one `<h1>` | Error | Blog posts are checked knowing the template supplies the heading |
| Heading levels don't skip | Warning | h2 → h4 with no h3. Reported once, at the first occurrence |
| Images have alt text | Warning | Counts images with **no alt attribute at all**. A deliberate `alt=""` is correct for decoration and is not flagged |
| Description present and sensible | Error / warning | Missing, under 50 characters, or over 160 |
| Answer comes first | Note | A question-shaped heading whose first paragraph runs past 60 words |
| Duplicate titles and descriptions | Warning | The same text on more than one page |

That "answer comes first" check is the genuinely AI-specific one. An answer
engine quotes the passage directly under a question. If that passage is a long
wind-up, it quotes the wind-up.

### What it checks site-wide

Missing business name · no profiles elsewhere · no logo · AI crawler rules
switched off · **front page is not a static page** (error) · **permalinks
still set to plain** (error) · Rank Math is managing robots.txt.

The last two are the ones that silently sabotage everything else.

### Limits, stated on the screen

The report covers your **200 most recent posts**, and says so where you can
see it. A report that quietly stops counting reads as "everything is fine".

Results are cached for 15 minutes and refresh when you edit a page. There is a
**Re-check** button.
